# Team Project Overview

The team project is **40% of the final grade** in [[Fundamentals of Software Engineering]]. Teams of **4**, all in the **same section**. All work is frontend and backend development of new features for **GameNite**, in TypeScript and React.

The individual deliverables each have their own note: [[Team Formation Survey (tp1)]] · [[Preliminary Project Plan (tp2)]] · [[Revised Project Plan (tp3)]] · [[Project Final Deliverable (tp4)]] · [[Individual Reflection (tp5)]].

| 2026-10-02 | Team Formation Survey | Specify preferences for teammates |
| 2026-10-05 (week of) | Project Kick-off Meeting | Schedule with your Mentor TA **this week** |
| 2026-10-16 | Preliminary Project Plan | Propose a feature implementable in 5 weeks |

## Grading Breakdown

| Weight | Component | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20% | **Planning** | Preliminary + Revised Project Plans |
| 20% | **Process** | Structured development process: regular git commits, pull requests, code reviews, timely progress reports and surveys, weekly TA mentor meetings. Also roughly equal division of labor — **for full credit each member needs at least 2 commits per sprint**, even when pair programming, after the revised plan is submitted. |
| 40% | **Product** | 20% MVP delivered as defined in your plan · 10% desirable features delivered · 10% test suite |
| 20% | **Reports** | 10% Final Report · 10% Poster and Demo |
| — | **Individual Reflection** | Required to receive an 'A' |

Unequal contribution can cost an individual up to **50%** of the team project grade — up to 100% for not contributing at all. Judged on weekly progress reports, version control history, self-reflection, and peer evaluations (collected in Weeks 6 and 8–13).

## Team Formation

The instructors assign teams based on your self-assessment and teammate preference survey, plus diversity of skills. You indicate preferences; you don't pick your own team outright.

## TA Mentor

Each team gets a TA mentor who works with you for the whole project and is your point of contact for project questions and grading.

- **Week 5** — kickoff meeting: meet your mentor, float early ideas before the preliminary plan
- **Weekly thereafter** — progress check-ins scheduled at your team's and the TA's convenience, often including review of your pull requests, commits, code reviews, and demos

## Preliminary Project Plan

Decide what feature to build — something implementable in the 4–5 week window, in a fork of the main codebase. Because you'll be up to speed on the codebase and have a team of 3–4, **the expectation is that your feature is more complex than the individual project features**.

Two sections:

1. **User stories and conditions of satisfaction** describing the features. Each condition prioritized **Essential / Desirable / Extension**. The essential set constitutes the MVP. *Teams are responsible for all essential **and** desirable work.* See [[Module 01 - Orientation and User Stories]].
2. **Work breakdown** — map user stories to engineering tasks. Assign each task to a member (or pair), estimate how long it takes, give a brief rationale for the estimate, and schedule the stories into sprints.

### Sprints

```
Sprint 0   Oct 16 – Oct 30
Sprint 1   Oct 31 – Nov 6   (single week)
Sprint 2   Nov 7  – Nov 20
Sprint 3   Nov 21 – Dec 4
```

## GitHub Repository

Development happens in a private repository in the course GitHub Classroom, visible only to your team and staff. After the semester you get full administrative control and may make it public. Setup instructions and starter code arrive **after revised project plans are submitted**.

## Revised Project Plan

Based on staff feedback, produce a more detailed plan:

- Revised user stories and conditions of satisfaction
- Revised work breakdown

Your team then self-organizes, as agile teams do, using the work breakdown and schedule as the basis for weekly TA check-ins.

## Software Development Process

Expected throughout: pull requests and code reviews for regular commits, roughly equal division of labor, regular progress reports / sprint retrospectives, and honest feedback on individual and team surveys.

> One of the most important factors in successfully completing a team project is open, honest, and effective communication between all team members as well as stakeholders.

## Final Deliverable

A **release** of your new feature on GitHub, with tests, accompanied by a demo:

- Implementation of the new features
- Automated tests for them
- Deployment of the application
- A Final Report
- A Poster & Demo (specifics vary by section)

Detailed rubrics are released by **Week 9**.

### Individual Reflection

Submitted separately by every student:

- **Evolution of the concept** — how does what you delivered compare to what you planned? What caused the deviations?
- **Process** — what software engineering processes do you wish, in hindsight, you had followed, or followed better?
- **Team dynamic** — a frank and ideally blameless postmortem of your and your teammates' collaboration. If you did this same project again with the same people, what would *you* do differently?
