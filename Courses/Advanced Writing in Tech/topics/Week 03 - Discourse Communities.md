## How the Three Fit Together

Swales gives the vocabulary — six criteria for what counts as a discourse community — and admits
at the end that his model is "utopian," free of the conflicts of a real university. Schryer walks
straight into that gap: one veterinary college, nominally one community, split in two by the two
genres its members write. Turkle and Yee then supply the method rather than the theory, which is
why they're assigned the same week as the archive activity: an object can carry an argument, and
annotating one is how you make it do so.

The through-line across all three is a hierarchy of knowing — abstract over concrete, research
over practice, the formal write-up over the messy work it reverses. Swales names the lexis that
marks insiders, Schryer shows which genre gets called knowledge, Turkle traces why the concrete
was demoted in the first place.

All of this is the toolkit for [[Assignment 1 - Discourse Community Archive]]: Swales for the
terms to bold, Schryer for a complication worth writing about, Yee for what a good annotation
looks like.

### Wed — Swales and Schryer discussion

Freewrite and discussion prompts:

- What discourse community are you part of?
- Can you describe how it meets the **six marks** of a discourse community?
- What genres do you write with your discourse community?
- Do you see these tensions between discourse communities in your own disciplines? (Her examples:
  lab vs. clinic, **finance vs. marketing**.)
- Why do you think these tensions between discourse communities and their genres exist?

The first three prompts are the first three things [[Assignment 1 - Discourse Community Archive]]
needs you to be able to answer.

### Thu — Archive activity: objects, data, and writing

- *So… what does it mean to build an archive?*
- *What does archival writing teach us about writing for discourse communities?*
- **The Pioneer Plaques and the Golden Record** are the model: objects built to describe a
  community (humanity) to a reader with **no shared context at all**. Prompt: *what object would
  you use to describe your discourse community during first contact?*

> **lexis**.

## Key Takeaways

- The six marks are the checklist; Schryer is the complication. One nominal community can split
  along the genres its members write.
- An archive is writing for outsiders about insiders. The Pioneer Plaque is the extreme case.
- The clay object in class is a rehearsal for, and possibly a piece of, Assignment 1.
