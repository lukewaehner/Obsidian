## Overarching Theme

Polish: 

Dota 2 is game with deeply rooted history built up from 15 years of lifespan, and 23 years since it's predecessor's inception. Over the years, plenty of objects have emerged. The common theme between all the objects is that they create **a standard of knowledge needed for membership**. There is no inherit inclusivity or exclusivity barring the outside from garnishing the same effects that the object procures to the players and community, but the complexities of the game make most objects innately foreign. This serves as a divide between the outsiders and insiders, without experience or understanding of the mechanics and references cementing these objects, the unknowing participant would have no comprehensive ability in regards to the objects ad their meanings.  The objects facing the outside world, like the map, memes, casters, or aegis only unravel meaning and importance when one understands what they specifically mean to the game. The importance of the map, the comedy behind the memes, or the prowess the aegis brings. They all rely on understanding if the game state itself. The text resources have created standards of approach in understanding. Each patch note is read in a specific way, and guild tags can instill different emotions when looked at from different sides. All of these objects come together to form a tightly knit system or history, belonging, and understanding. In participation with the community, each objects shrouded barrier falls, they all begin to link together and form a web of meaning for the player. One that both connect them to others, and to the history and meaning behind the game itself.

## The Seven Objects

1. The Map
One of the most iconic and memorable features of the game itself is the map. Left, for the majority, unchanged for three years has buried itself with history. 

The map serves as a lexis, it gives dictation to many points of interest around the map that are referenced frequently in discussion, safe lane, offlane, triangle, tier 2 towers, runes, the river. They only have meaning if you know the map, reference of any of these topics mean nothing without knowing the map. Further, players act through the map itself its a division of roles and labor, thus a meditating object. The specific roles of the game (that are self imposed by the community, not a standard) are defined by the location of your character throughout the map in the early stages of a match. The map is stablizied for now, the patches can update the map, changing terrain, camp locations, tower strength, width, depth, locations of the bosses, and more. The community must relearn the derived efficiency patterns and best practiced upon updates. Kairos as well, chronological rune spawns, rosh timers, and lotus pools, even watchers are built into the map itself on a time based system.
2. The Aegis of Champions vs Aegis of the Immortal
Ceremonials - picking up the item in game is standardized, and apart of every strategy realistically, the picking up the physical trophy is completely different. One is commemorated for years to come, the other is forgotten the next day. Boundary object as the item means extra life in game, while the trophy means world champion. Biography of things with the team names being physically engraved into the same object that is kept year over year, it's given a story and physically represents history. 
3. Patch Notes
Captures, fixed reading routines going from the synopsis to favorite heroes, common enemies, checking meta changes, items, and a a general scanned. Repeated patches have developed a reading system. Each reader gravitates to sections in the same manner, but reads in different styles. Dialogic, each patch answers the prior, there are constant shifts in power dynamics for individual heroes, the game state and the consensus of 'best strategy' is changed on a weekly basis (cadenced to patch releases). Community's response, not just the notes is what defines the meta. There are many times where meta's are discovered late in the cycle of a game state, and removed later, while some are obvious with over-tuning of abilities, items, matchups, etcetera. Stabilized for now (if need more space) - meta lasts until the next patch, where it shifts and is redrawn while the updates
4. Memes
322 or RTZ cliff or Godson carries the entirety of the event with it, people who were not around to see some of the original inceptions of the specific moments that define the memes know of their existence because of the use of the meme. Call and response, spamming in chat allows the community to commemorate the moment, or dictate a type of event through a reference to a synonymic meme. Curation is authorship - the gamemakers choose the moments that become voice lines, sprays, chatwheel lines - what gets officially remembered . Lexis, not knowing the meme makes you an outsider
5. The guild tag
Uptake 'oh no, the Naga clan' or 'classic Naga throw' is something I have seen in game myself, the opponents build the guild's reputation as much as the guild itself. Addressivity, the tag is shown to players before the chat feature is even enabled, no direct communication line is established and there is already communication between the teams. Boundary object, friend and belonging to the ones in the guilds, a potential threat or a running joke to the opponent seeing the tag in game. Objects as parts of self, the tag is how I am memorable in the NA server, I am known as being a member of a specific clan
6. Rank (mmr / medals)
Power, rank turns community knowledge into a number that grants rights, like claiming a specific position or hero over someone else. Rules, community, division of labor "it's just the rules" whilst not being official at all, is what dictates who is allowed to play or do what. Which genre counts as knowledge, lower ranks are dismissed because of their standing. Lexis, medal names are insults for insiders, being  told one is a Harold is a direct insult, but without the knowledge of the ranking system an outsider is unaware
7. Casting and broadcasters
Community-engaged technical communicator, casters are the DJ figures of the game, they operate in a way where an insider of any level is able to understand what they are referencing, but it is localized - someone who has never seen the game before will not understand what is happening. Multimodal voice, replays, graphics, stat overlays, and chat make meaning all together. Ceremonials, the same match means more cast at TI than in a stream of a pub match. Kairos, casters time hype and explanations to the moment a fight breaks out, the boring parts of the map are not engaged by the casters, used to show stat lines, predictions, talk about overall strategies, etc. The hype moments go quickly - play by play of the fight, specific vocal choices, etc.
