

Mutual funds that invest primarily in fixed-income securities, offering income generation with varying risk levels.

## Classification Dimensions

Bond funds are distinguished by five main characteristics:

### 1. Maturity Range
- [[Short-Term Bond Funds]] - 1-3 years
- [[Intermediate-Term Bond Funds]] - 3-10 years
- Long-term bond funds - 10+ years

**Why it matters:**
- Longer maturity = higher interest rate risk
- Longer maturity = typically higher yields
- Affects price volatility

### 2. Taxability
- **Taxable bond funds** - Corporate bonds, Treasuries
- **Tax-exempt funds** - Municipal bonds
- [[Single-State Municipal Funds]] - State-specific munis

**Tax considerations:**
- Municipal bonds exempt from federal tax
- State-specific munis may be exempt from state tax too
- Taxable vs. tax-exempt comparison depends on tax bracket

### 3. Bond Type
- Treasury bond funds - U.S. government
- Corporate bond funds - Company debt
- [[Mortgage Bond Funds]] - Mortgage-backed securities
- [[General Bond Funds]] - Multiple types
- Agency bond funds - Government agencies

### 4. Issuing Country
- Domestic bond funds - U.S. only
- [[World Bond Funds]] - International exposure
- Emerging market bond funds - Developing countries

**Currency risk:**
- Foreign bonds add currency exposure
- Can enhance returns or increase volatility

### 5. Credit Quality
- Investment-grade funds - Higher quality (BBB- or above)
- [[High-Yield Bond Funds]] - Lower quality (below BBB-)
- [[Insured Bond Funds]] - Additional credit protection

## Major Fund Types

### By Risk/Return Profile

**Lower Risk:**
- Short-term Treasury funds
- Short-term investment-grade corporate
- [[Insured Bond Funds]]

**Moderate Risk:**
- [[Intermediate-Term Bond Funds]]
- Investment-grade corporate
- [[Mortgage Bond Funds]]
- [[General Bond Funds]]

**Higher Risk:**
- [[High-Yield Bond Funds]] (junk bonds)
- Long-term bond funds
- Emerging market bonds
- [[World Bond Funds]] (currency risk)

### Special Categories

**[[Single-State Municipal Funds]]**
- Focus on one state's municipal bonds
- Double tax-exempt for state residents
- Lower diversification
- Higher state-specific risk

**[[High-Yield Bond Funds]]**
- Also called "junk bond funds"
- Below investment grade (< BBB-)
- Higher yields, higher default risk
- More equity-like behavior

**[[Mortgage Bond Funds]]**
- Invest in mortgage-backed securities
- Government agency MBS (lower risk)
- Private label MBS (higher risk)
- Prepayment risk

**[[General Bond Funds]]**
- Diversified across bond types
- Manager has flexibility
- Balanced approach

**[[World Bond Funds]]**
- International bond exposure
- Currency risk/opportunity
- Diversification benefits

**[[Insured Bond Funds]]**
- Bonds have credit insurance
- Lower yields for lower risk
- Insurance quality matters

## Income vs. Total Return Focus

### Income-Oriented
- Prioritize current yield
- Less concerned with price changes
- Suitable for retirees
- Lower turnover

### Total Return-Oriented
- Balance income and capital appreciation
- More active management
- Higher turnover
- Better in certain rate environments

## Interest Rate Sensitivity

### Duration Matters
- **Short duration** (1-3 years) - Low rate sensitivity
- **Intermediate duration** (3-7 years) - Moderate sensitivity  
- **Long duration** (7+ years) - High rate sensitivity

### Rate Scenarios
- **Rising rates** → Bond prices fall
  - Short-term funds hurt less
  - Long-term funds hurt more
  
- **Falling rates** → Bond prices rise
  - Long-term funds benefit more
  - Short-term funds benefit less

## Selection Considerations

### Choosing Bond Funds
Consider:
1. **Income needs** - Current vs. total return
2. **Time horizon** - Match to maturity range
3. **Tax situation** - Taxable vs. municipal
4. **Risk tolerance** - Investment grade vs. high yield
5. **Interest rate outlook** - Duration positioning
6. **Credit risk comfort** - Quality level
7. **Diversification** - Complement to stock holdings

### Tax Bracket Analysis
**When to use municipal bond funds:**
```
Tax-Equivalent Yield = Municipal Yield / (1 - Tax Rate)
```
If tax-equivalent yield > taxable yield, choose municipal

**Example:**
- Municipal fund yields 3%
- Your tax rate is 35%
- Tax-equivalent yield = 3% / (1 - 0.35) = 4.62%
- Choose municipal if taxable funds yield < 4.62%

## Role in Portfolio

### Diversification Benefits
- Lower correlation with stocks
- Income generation
- Portfolio stability
- Downside protection

### Common Allocations
- Young investors: 10-30% bonds
- Middle-age investors: 30-50% bonds
- Near-retirement: 40-60% bonds
- Retirees: 50-70% bonds

*But see [[Target Date Funds]] for age-based allocation strategies*

## Risk Factors

### Interest Rate Risk
- Bond prices move inversely with rates
- Longer maturities = higher risk
- Duration measures sensitivity

### Credit Risk
- Default risk on corporate/muni bonds
- Higher in high-yield funds
- Lower in Treasury funds

### Inflation Risk
- Fixed payments lose purchasing power
- Real returns can be negative
- TIPS funds address this

### Liquidity Risk
- Some bonds hard to sell
- Smaller issue sizes
- Less liquid market segments

### Call Risk
- Bonds may be called away
- Reinvestment at lower rates
- Affects some municipal bonds
