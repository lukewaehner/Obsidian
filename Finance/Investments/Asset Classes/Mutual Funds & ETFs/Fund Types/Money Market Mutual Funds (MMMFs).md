

**Definition:** Mutual funds that specialize in [[Money Market Instruments]], designed to function like cash accounts.

## Key Characteristics

### Investment Focus
- Invest in short-term, high-quality debt securities
- Money market instruments include:
  - Treasury bills
  - Commercial paper
  - Certificates of deposit
  - Repurchase agreements

### Historical NAV Policy
- All MMMFs **used to** maintain a **$1.00 net asset value**
- Goal: Resemble bank accounts (stable value)
- Made them feel like cash accounts to investors

### Important Disclaimer
⚠**There is NO guarantee that NAV will be $1.00 or more**

### Breaking the Buck
**Definition:** When a MMMF's [[Net Asset Value (NAV)]] falls below $1.00

- Rare but possible event
- Signals problems with fund's holdings
- Can trigger panic and redemptions
- Most famous case: Reserve Primary Fund (2008 financial crisis)

See: [[Breaking the Buck]]

## Types of MMMFs

### By Tax Treatment

1. **Taxable MMMFs**
   - Invest in taxable money market securities
   - Interest income is fully taxable
   - Higher yields than tax-exempt

2. **Tax-Exempt MMMFs**
   - Invest in short-term municipal securities
   - Interest income may be exempt from federal (and sometimes state) taxes
   - Lower yields than taxable funds
   - Attractive for high-tax-bracket investors

## Use Cases

### When to Use MMMFs
- Emergency funds
- Short-term savings
- Cash management
- Temporary parking of funds between investments
- Alternative to bank savings accounts

### Advantages
- Higher yields than typical bank accounts (historically)
- High liquidity
- Professional management
- Diversification across multiple securities
- Check-writing privileges (many funds)

### Limitations
- Not FDIC insured (unlike bank accounts)
- Yields fluctuate with interest rates
- Small risk of "breaking the buck"
- Subject to [[Operating Expenses|fund expenses]]

## Regulatory Changes

Post-2008 Financial Crisis:
- Increased regulation of MMMFs
- Some institutional funds can have floating NAVs
- Gates and fees possible in stress scenarios
- Enhanced disclosure requirements

## Relationship to Other Fund Types

**Categorization:**
- Classified as **short-term funds**
- Distinct from:
  - [[Stock Funds]] (long-term)
  - [[Bond Funds]] (long-term)
  - [[Balanced Funds]] (long-term)

**Risk Profile:**
- Lowest risk among mutual fund types
- But NOT risk-free
- Higher risk than FDIC-insured bank deposits
