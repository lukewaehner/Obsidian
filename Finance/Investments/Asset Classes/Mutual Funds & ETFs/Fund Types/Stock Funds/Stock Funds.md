Mutual funds that invest primarily in equities, offering various approaches to stock market exposure.

## Investment Objective Spectrum

Stock funds trade off between **capital appreciation** and **dividend income**:

### 1. [[Capital Appreciation Funds]]
- **Focus:** Maximum capital gains
- **Strategy:** Aggressive growth
- **Income:** Minimal or no dividends
- **Risk:** Highest among stock funds

### 2. [[Growth Funds]]
- **Focus:** Capital appreciation
- **Strategy:** Growth-oriented companies
- **Income:** Low dividends
- **Risk:** Moderate-high

### 3. [[Growth and Income Funds]]
- **Focus:** Balance of appreciation and income
- **Strategy:** Mix of growth and dividend stocks
- **Income:** Moderate dividends
- **Risk:** Moderate

### 4. [[Equity Income Funds]]
- **Focus:** Current income from dividends
- **Strategy:** High-dividend stocks
- **Income:** Substantial dividends
- **Risk:** Lower (for stock funds)

## Classification by Company Size

Market capitalization-based segmentation:

### By Market Cap
- [[Small-Cap Funds]] - Small companies, higher risk/return potential
- [[Mid-Cap Funds]] - Medium companies, balanced approach
- [[Large-Cap Funds]] - Large established companies, more stable

**Why Size Matters:**
- Different risk/return profiles
- Different growth potential
- Different liquidity characteristics
- Different economic sensitivity

## Geographic Focus

### International Exposure
- [[Global Funds]] - Worldwide, including U.S.
- [[International Funds]] - Worldwide, excluding U.S.
- [[Regional Funds]] - Specific regions (e.g., Europe, Asia)
- [[Country Funds]] - Single country focus
- [[Emerging Markets Funds]] - Developing economies

## Sector Specialization

### [[Sector Funds]]
Specialize in specific sectors of the economy:
- **Technology** - Internet, software, hardware
- **Energy** - Oil, gas, renewables
- **Healthcare** - Pharmaceuticals, biotech
- **Financial Services** - Banks, insurance
- **Consumer** - Retail, consumer goods
- **Real Estate** - REITs and real estate companies

**Characteristics:**
- Higher risk (lack of diversification)
- Potential for outsized returns
- More volatile than diversified funds
- Requires market timing or strong conviction

## Special Strategy Funds

### [[Index Funds]]
- Track specific market indices
- Passive management
- Lower fees
- Broad diversification

### [[Social Conscience Funds]] (Green Funds)
- ESG criteria (Environmental, Social, Governance)
- Exclude certain industries
- Focus on sustainable companies
- May sacrifice some returns for values

### [[Sin Funds]]
- Opposite of social conscience funds
- Invest in tobacco, liquor, gaming, etc.
- "Vice funds"
- Sometimes outperform due to industry dynamics

### [[Tax-Managed Funds]]
- Minimize taxable distributions
- Tax-efficient trading strategies
- Avoid short-term capital gains
- Better for taxable accounts

## Selection Considerations

### Choosing Stock Funds
Consider:
1. **Investment objective** - Growth vs. income
2. **Risk tolerance** - Aggressive vs. conservative
3. **Time horizon** - Long-term allows more risk
4. **Diversification needs** - Broad vs. sector-specific
5. **Tax situation** - Taxable vs. tax-advantaged accounts
6. **Geographic preference** - Domestic vs. international
7. **Company size preference** - Small vs. large cap

### Risk Hierarchy (Generally)
**Higher Risk →**
1. Small-cap growth
2. Sector funds
3. Emerging markets
4. International growth
5. Large-cap growth
6. Global balanced
7. Large-cap value
8. Equity income
**← Lower Risk**

## Performance Factors

### What Drives Returns
- Market conditions
- Manager skill (active funds)
- Sector allocation
- Geographic allocation
- Size exposure
- Style (growth vs. value)
- [[Turnover Rate|Trading activity]]
- [[Expense Ratio|Fees and expenses]]

### Evaluation Metrics
- Total return
- Risk-adjusted return (Sharpe ratio)
- Benchmark comparison
- Consistency over time
- Expense ratio
- Manager tenure
