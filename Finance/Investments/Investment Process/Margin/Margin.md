## Definition
Borrowing money from a broker to purchase securities.

$$
\text{Margin} = \frac{\text{Equity}}{\text{Stock Value}}
$$
## Components
- Initial Margin: minimum % investor must put up
- Maintenance Margin: minimum equity to avoid margin call
- Margin Call: demand to deposit more funds
- Return: magnified compared to no leverage

Borrowing is not free
The portion that is borrowed incurs an interest charge.
- This interest is based on the broker's **call money rate**
- The call money rate is the reate brokers pay to borrow money to lend to customers in their margin account

## Initial Margin
- In a margin purchase, the amount that must be supplied, or the minimum margin. 
## Maintenance Margin:
- The margin amount that must be present at all times in a margin account.
## Margin Call
- When the margin drops below the maintenance margin, the broker can demand more funds. This is known as a **margin call**.

### The Balance Sheet


**Step 1: Cost of Shares**
-   1,000 shares × \$39 = ****\$39,000****
- 
**Step 2: Amount Put Up (Equity)**
-   You put in ****\$30,000****

**Step 3: Amount Borrowed**
-   \$39,000 (total) − \$30,000 (equity) = ****\$9,000****

**Step 4: Margin**
	Formula:
	`Margin = Account Equity / Value of Stock`
	Calculation: 
	 Margin = 30,000 ÷ 39,000 ≈ 0.769 (76.9%)

**Balance Sheet**
***Assets*** - 1,000 Shares, PFE → \$39,000\
- ***Total Assets = \$39,000***
***Liabilities and Account Equity*** - Margin Loan → \$9,000
- Account Equity → \$30,000
- ***Total = \$39,000***

---  

***Final Answers*** - Amount Borrowed = ***\$9,000***
- Margin = ***76.9%***
