## Prospect Theory

A behavioral economic theory describing how people evaluate gains and losses asymmetrically. Developed by Kahneman and Tversky, it challenges the traditional [[Utility Function|utility theory]] assumption of rational decision-making.

---

## Core Insight

A $100 loss hurts more than a $100 gain feels good.

The value function in prospect theory is:

- Concave for gains (diminishing sensitivity to increasing gains)
- Convex for losses (diminishing sensitivity to increasing losses)
- Steeper for losses than for gains (loss aversion)

---

## Example

Consider two investors who each have $10,000:

Investor A gains $1,000, then loses $1,000, ending at $10,000.
Investor B has no change, staying at $10,000.

Economically, both end at the same place. But Investor A feels worse because the pain of the $1,000 loss outweighed the pleasure of the $1,000 gain. This explains why investors may irrationally avoid volatility even when expected returns are identical.

---

## Implications for Investing

- Investors may hold losing stocks too long (hoping to avoid realizing the painful loss)
- Investors may sell winning stocks too early (locking in the pleasure of a gain)
- Portfolios may be suboptimal due to emotional rather than rational decisions
