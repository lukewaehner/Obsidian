## Recency Bias

The tendency to give recent events disproportionate weight in decision-making, while underweighting older but potentially more relevant information.

---

## How It Affects Investors

Investors with recency bias:

- Project recent performance trends into the future
- Overreact to short-term market movements
- Chase recent winners and sell recent losers
- Forget lessons from past market cycles

---

## Example

After a strong bull market lasting three years, an investor allocates 90% of their portfolio to stocks, reasoning that equities "always go up." They have forgotten or discounted the 2008 financial crisis and 2020 COVID crash because those events feel distant. When a correction occurs, they panic sell at the bottom, having been psychologically unprepared for normal market volatility.

---

## Another Example

In early 2022, an investor avoids bonds entirely because bond returns have been poor for several years with rising rates. They miss that bonds historically provide diversification during equity downturns. Their recency bias toward recent poor bond performance blinds them to bonds' longer-term portfolio role.
