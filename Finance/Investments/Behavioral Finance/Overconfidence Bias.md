## Overconfidence Bias

A cognitive bias where investors overestimate their abilities and the precision of their beliefs or forecasts.

---

## Manifestations

Overconfident investors tend to:

- Believe they can beat the market consistently
- Underestimate the uncertainty in their predictions
- Trade more frequently than warranted
- Hold concentrated portfolios rather than diversifying

---

## Example

An investor analyzes a company and predicts its stock will rise 20% over the next year. They are so confident in their analysis that they invest 40% of their portfolio in this single stock. When asked how confident they are, they say 90%. Studies show that when investors claim 90% confidence, they are actually correct only about 60-70% of the time. This overconfidence leads to under-diversification and excessive risk-taking.

---

## Market Impact

Overconfidence contributes to:

- Excessive trading volume in markets
- Higher transaction costs eroding returns
- Persistence of [[Systematic and Unsystematic Risk|unsystematic risk]] in portfolios
