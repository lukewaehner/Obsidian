## Attention and Heuristics

Individuals have limited time and attention, leading them to rely on mental shortcuts (heuristics) rather than thorough analysis.

---

## Consequences of Limited Attention

Investors operating under attention constraints tend to:

- Overreact to attention-grabbing news (dramatic headlines, large price moves)
- Underreact to news that seems mundane but is economically significant
- React to unrelated news that happens to be salient
- Trade stocks that are "in the news" regardless of fundamentals

---

## Example

A technology company announces both a CEO resignation (attention-grabbing) and a 2% increase in operating margins (buried in an earnings report footnote). Investors react strongly to the CEO news, causing the stock to drop 8%. The margin improvement, which may have greater long-term value impact, is largely ignored because it requires careful reading of financial statements. A patient investor who focuses on the margin improvement rather than the headline may find an opportunity.

---

## Another Example

During earnings season, stocks reporting after the market close receive less attention than those reporting before market open, because investors have limited bandwidth. Studies show after-hours announcers have more delayed price reactions, creating potential inefficiencies.
