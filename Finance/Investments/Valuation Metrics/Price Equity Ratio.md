# Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio

## Definition
The P/E ratio measures a company’s current share price relative to its earnings per share (EPS). It is a common valuation metric in finance.

$$
P/E = \frac{\text{Price per Share}}{\text{Earnings per Share (EPS)}}
$$

## Purpose
- Indicates how much investors are willing to pay per dollar of earnings.
- Useful for comparing valuation across companies in the same industry.
- Can reflect market expectations about a company’s future growth.

## Types
- **Trailing P/E**: Uses earnings from the past 12 months.
- **Forward P/E**: Uses estimated future earnings.

## Interpretation
- **High P/E**: May indicate high growth expectations, or that the stock is overvalued.
- **Low P/E**: May indicate undervaluation, or that the company has weak growth prospects.

## Limitations
- Not useful for companies with negative earnings.
- Different industries have different typical P/E ranges.
- Does not account for growth rates, debt, or other fundamentals.
