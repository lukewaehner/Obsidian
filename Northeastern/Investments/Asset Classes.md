## Interest Bearing Asset

---
### Money market instruments
Short-term debt obligations of large corporations and governments
- promised one future payment
- lives are less than one year
	- Ex: T-bills
	- Money market funds
	- CDs, corporate and municipal money market instruments
You only lose on borrowers defaulting
Instruments are sold on a discount basis, only the interest rates are quoted
Investors must be able to calculate prices from the quoted rates

---
### Fixed-income securities
Longer-term debt obligations of corporations and governments.
- Promise to make fixed payments on a pre-set schedule
	- Lives extend one year
	- Long-term treasury bond index bond
	- US government 20-year bond
	- Corporate bonds, auto asset-backed securities, student loans backed securities
Fixed coupon payments and final payment at maturity, except defaulting
Possibility of gain(loss) from fall(rise) in interest rates.
Depending on the debt issue, **illiquidity** can be a problem


> If you cannot sell securities quickly for their current market value, the market is said to be illiquid

**Exercise**:
100 bonds @ $1,000 FV that cost $900 each.
Coupon rate of 10% semiannually, maturing in 5 years

$$
\$ 1000 \times \frac{10\%}{2}
$$

At maturity you get, $1000 FV + $50 coupon, so $1050, and 100 times that

Timeline:
- @ Purchase (-900)
- 0.5 year: +50
- 1.0 year: + 50
- {..0.5}: + 50
- 5 year: + 1050

#  Stocks:

---
Equity based assets
## Preferred:
- Fixed dividend
- Higher priority than common shareholders for dividends
## Common:
- Residual ownership in a corporation
	- receives a pro rated share of whatever is left over after all obligations are met, in the event of liquidation
- Interim Cash Flows through dividends, timing or amount is not guaranteed unless announced
- Capital Gain / Losses: value may rise or fall depending on company prospects and market-wide circumstances
- **Bid**: price people want to buy at
- **Ask**: price people want to sell at
- **Volume**: the amount of shares traded in a given day
- **Market Capitalization**: shares outstanding x share price 
	- ($10B+) = Large cap
	- ($2-10B) = Med cap
	- ($300M-$2B) = Small cap
- **Earning date**: the public date where a company announces financial performances for a specific period
- **Earnings Per Share**: 
- **P/E Ratio**: Price per share / Earnings per share
	- E.G. PE: 20 -> $20 invested for every $1 of the earning
	- Higher PE ratio means the company is overvalued, growth stocks

# Priority Claims in Liquidation
1. Secured creditors - secured bonds, loans, debt
2. Trustee's administrative costs
3. Expenses incurred after involuntary case begun but before trustee appointed
4. Wage due workers
5. Unpaid contribution to employee benefit plans
6. Unsecured claims for customer deposits
7. Taxes due
8. Unfunded pension plan
9. General (unsecured) creditors - unsecured bonds, debt
10. Preferred stockholders
11. Common stockholders
