#margin

---
- You short 100 shares of TSLA at $400 per share
- Your broker has 50% initial margin, and a 40$ maintenance margin on short sales
- You need to make deposit to have 50% initial margin.

---

> Margin = Equity / Value of Short Position

The value of stock borrowed that will be sold short is:


| Assets                                              | Liabilities and Account Equity                            |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sale Proceeds: $40,000                              | Liability (Short Position): $\$400 \times \$100 = 40,000$ |
| Initial Margin Deposit: $50\% \times 40,000=20,000$ | Equity: $\$20,000$ initial deposit                        |
| Sum: $\$60,000$                                     | Sum: $\$60,000$                                           |

---
### Fixed vs Variable Values
Fixed:
- You already have gained $40,000
- You already had to deposit $20,000

Variable:
- Short Position - You have to give the shares back at whatever price they currently run at. If they move up to $500, you have to buy $50,000 worth of shares.
- Equity - You gain equity if you pay less for each share, you lose equity if you have to pay more.
