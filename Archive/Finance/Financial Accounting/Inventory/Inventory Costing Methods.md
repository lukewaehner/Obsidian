## Inventory Costing Methods

When specific identification is impractical, use cost flow assumptions.

### Specific Identification

- Identifies the specific unit sold
- Used for limited variety, high unit-cost items (planes, custom furniture, art)

### FIFO (First-In, First-Out)

- Earliest goods purchased are first to be sold
- Most recent purchases stay in ending inventory
- In rising prices: Higher ending inventory, lower COGS, higher net income

### LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)

- Last batch purchased is first to be sold
- Oldest purchases stay in ending inventory
- In rising prices: Lower ending inventory, higher COGS, lower net income
- Not allowed under IFRS

### Average Cost Method

$$
\text{Weighted Average Cost} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Available for Sale}}{\text{Total Units Available for Sale}}
$$

## Example

| Date | Units | Cost | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginning | 100 | $130 | $13,000 |
| Purchase 1 | 150 | $160 | $24,000 |
| Purchase 2 | 110 | $180 | $21,600 |
| **Total** | **360** | | **$58,800** |

Ending inventory: 75 units

**FIFO**: Ending Inventory = 75 × $180 = $13,500; COGS = $58,800 - $13,500 = $45,300

**LIFO**: Ending Inventory = 75 × $130 = $9,750; COGS = $58,800 - $9,750 = $49,050

**Average**: Cost per unit = $58,800 / 360 = $163.33; Ending Inventory = 75 × $163.33 = $12,250

---

## Consistency

Companies must use one cost flow method consistently. Changes require significant disclosures.
