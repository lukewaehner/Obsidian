## Depreciation Methods

The process of allocating the cost of a plant asset over its useful life.

### Accumulated Depreciation

- Contra-asset account
- Represents total cost charged to expense to date

$$
\text{Net Book Value} = \text{Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}
$$

---

## Straight-Line Method

Most widely used. Same depreciation each year.

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Useful Life}}
$$

**Example:**

Truck: Cost $22,000, Residual Value $2,000, Useful Life 5 years

Annual Depreciation = ($22,000 - $2,000) / 5 = $4,000/year

---

## Units-of-Activity Method

Based on actual usage. Ideal for factory machines, vehicles, airplanes.

$$
\text{Depreciation per Unit} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Total Units of Activity}}
$$

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \text{Depreciation per Unit} \times \text{Units Used}
$$

**Example:**

Taxi: Cost $27,500, Salvage $500, Expected 150,000 miles. Driven 20,000 miles in 2021.

Depreciation = ($27,500 - $500) / 150,000 × 20,000 = $3,600

---

## Declining Balance Method

Accelerated method. Higher depreciation in early years.

**Double-Declining Balance:**

$$
\text{Depreciation} = \text{Net Book Value} \times \frac{2}{\text{Useful Life}}
$$
