## Definition
EPS measures a company’s profitability on a per-share basis. It is the portion of a company’s profit allocated to each outstanding share of common stock.

$$
EPS = \frac{\text{Net Income – Preferred Dividends}}{\text{Average Outstanding Shares}}
$$

## Purpose
- Indicates how much profit is earned for each share.
- Commonly used in financial analysis and stock valuation.
- Key input for the [[P-E Ratio]].

## Types
- **Basic EPS**: Uses the actual number of outstanding shares.
- **Diluted EPS**: Accounts for potential dilution from stock options, warrants, or convertible securities.

## Interpretation
- Higher EPS generally indicates greater profitability.
- Growth in EPS over time is often a positive signal for investors.

## Limitations
- Can be manipulated through share buybacks or accounting methods.
- EPS growth does not always reflect underlying business strength if debt is used to boost earnings.
