

**Definition:** A commission or sales charge paid when purchasing mutual fund shares.

Also called: **Sales Load**, **Purchase Fee**

## Key Characteristics

### When Charged
- **At the time of purchase**
- One-time fee
- Deducted from your investment amount

### How It Works
```
Investment Amount = Money Invested - Front-End Load
Shares Received = Investment Amount / NAV
```

**Example:**
- You invest $10,000
- Front-end load is 5%
- Load charge = $10,000 × 5% = $500
- Amount actually invested = $10,000 - $500 = $9,500
- You receive shares worth $9,500 at NAV

### Who Receives the Load?
- Pays **brokers and financial advisors** who sell the funds
- Compensates them for advice and service
- Fund company distributes to selling agents

## Regulatory Limits

### Maximum Allowed
- **May not exceed 8.5%** by law
- In practice, rarely above **6%**
- Many loads now in 3-5.75% range

### Historical Context
- Loads were historically higher
- Competition has driven them down
- Many funds now offer no-load alternatives

## Load Breakpoints

### Volume Discounts
As investment size increases, load percentage decreases:

**Example structure:**
| Investment Amount | Front-End Load |
|-------------------|----------------|
| $0 - $24,999 | 5.75% |
| $25,000 - $49,999 | 4.75% |
| $50,000 - $99,999 | 3.75% |
| $100,000 - $249,999 | 2.50% |
| $250,000+ | 0% |

### Rights of Accumulation
- Combine existing holdings with new investments
- Qualify for lower load rates
- Applies across fund family

### Letters of Intent
- Commit to invest certain amount over time period
- Get lower load rate immediately
- Must fulfill commitment or pay difference

## Share Class Context

### [[Share Classes|Class A Shares]]
- Typically have **front-end loads**
- But **lower annual expenses** ([[12b-1 Fees]])
- Better for **long-term investors**

### Comparison
| Share Class | Front Load | 12b-1 Fee | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Class A | 4-5.75% | 0.25% | Long-term holders |
| Class C | 0% | 1% | Short-term or uncertain |
| Class I | 0% | 0% | Institutions ($5M+ min) |

## Impact on Returns

### Immediate Reduction
- Starts with less money working for you
- Must make up the load before breaking even
- Longer time horizon reduces annual impact

### Break-Even Analysis
With 5% front-end load:
- Year 1: Need 5.26% return just to break even
- Year 5: Need 1.02% annualized to break even
- Year 10: Need 0.51% annualized to break even

## When Front-End Loads Make Sense

### Potential Justifications
1. **Professional advice included**
2. **Lower ongoing expenses** (Class A vs C)
3. **Large investment amounts** (breakpoints)
4. **Fund family benefits**

## When to Avoid

- No advisory service provided
- Short investment time horizon
- Comparable no-load alternative exists
- Fund has poor performance history
