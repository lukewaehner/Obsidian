# Relevant Costs

Information for decision-making that focuses on costs and revenues that differ between alternatives.

## Core Concept

**Relevant information** must be:
1. **Future-oriented**: Relates to future decisions, not past
2. **Different between alternatives**: Varies based on the choice made

**If it doesn't meet BOTH criteria, it's not relevant for the decision.**

## Relevant vs Irrelevant Costs

### Relevant Costs (Differential/Avoidable Costs)

**Definition**: Costs that change depending on the decision made.

**Characteristics**:
- Future costs
- Differ between alternatives
- Can be avoided by choosing one alternative

**Examples**:
- Additional materials needed for special order
- Labor costs that change with decision
- Costs saved by discontinuing a product line

### Irrelevant Costs

**Two main types of irrelevant costs**:

#### 1. Sunk Costs

**Definition**: Costs incurred in past transactions that cannot be changed.

**Key Insight**: Already spent, decision can't change it.

**Examples**:
```
"I bought stock for $25,000, now worth $15,000"
  - The $25,000 is sunk
  - Can't get it back regardless of decision
  - Irrelevant for future decisions

Current decision:
  - Sell now: Get $15,000
  - Hold: Have investment worth $15,000
  - The relevant value is $15,000, not $25,000
```

**Common Sunk Costs**:
- Original purchase price of equipment
- Past research and development costs
- Prior advertising expenditures
- Depreciation on existing assets

#### 2. Costs That Don't Differ

**Definition**: Costs that are the same regardless of choice.

**Examples**:
- Factory rent (if keeping facility either way)
- Equipment depreciation (if keeping machine either way)
- Salaried manager (if keeping manager either way)

**Key Insight**: If it's the same for all alternatives, ignore it.

## Opportunity Costs

**Definition**: The benefit sacrificed when choosing one alternative over another.

**Formula**:
```
Opportunity Cost = Value of Next Best Alternative Given Up
```

### Example: Laptop Decision

**Situation**: Considering buying a laptop, think price will drop soon.

**Alternative 1**: Buy now for $1,000
**Alternative 2**: Wait, expect price to drop to $900

**Opportunity Cost of Waiting**:
- Can't use laptop until later
- Value of using laptop now = opportunity cost

**Opportunity Cost of Buying Now**:
- Pay $100 more than future price
- Opportunity cost = $100 savings foregone

**Decision**: Compare opportunity costs and benefits of each choice.

## Quantitative vs Qualitative Factors

### Quantitative Factors

**Definition**: Factors that can be measured in numerical terms.

**Examples**:
- Cost savings: $10,000 per year
- Additional revenue: $50,000
- Material cost: $5 per unit
- Labor time: 2 hours per unit

**Analysis**: Use calculations, formulas, financial models

### Qualitative Factors

**Definition**: Factors that matter but can't be easily quantified.

**Examples**:
- Employee morale
- Customer satisfaction
- Product quality
- Company reputation
- Strategic fit
- Environmental impact

### Laptop Example Revisited

**Quantitative**:
- Computer A costs $300 more than Computer B
- Both meet technical requirements

**Qualitative**:
- Computer A has more attractive appearance
- You derive greater utility from A's design

**Decision**: Despite higher cost, qualitative factor (appearance) may make Computer A worth the premium.

## Relevant Revenues

**Must also be**:
- Future-oriented
- Different between alternatives

**Examples**:

**Relevant Revenue**:
```
Special order: Accept = $50,000 revenue
               Reject = $0 revenue
Differential: $50,000 (relevant)
```

**Irrelevant Revenue**:
```
Regular sales: $1,000,000 regardless of special order decision
Differential: $0 (irrelevant)
```

## Avoidable Costs by Level

Different decisions affect different cost levels:

### Unit-Level Costs

**Avoidable by**: Eliminating one unit of product

**Examples**:
- Direct materials for one unit
- Packaging for one unit
- Direct labor for one unit (in some contexts)

### Batch-Level Costs

**Avoidable by**: Eliminating a batch of work

**Examples**:
- Machine setup costs
- Purchase order processing
- Material movement costs

### Product-Level Costs

**Avoidable by**: Eliminating entire product line

**Examples**:
- Product-specific advertising
- Product manager salary
- Product-specific equipment
- Product design and engineering

### Facility-Level Costs

**Avoidable by**: Eliminating entire business segment or facility

**Examples**:
- Factory rent (if can sublease or eliminate)
- Factory manager salary (if position eliminated)
- Facility property taxes

**Note**: Facility-level costs are often **NOT avoidable** in short-term decisions.

## Accuracy vs Relevance

**Important Principle**: Information doesn't need to be exact to be relevant.

**Example**: Price Decrease Expectation
```
Thinking of buying laptop now vs waiting

Expected price decrease:
  - Don't know exact amount
  - But believe some cost can be avoided by waiting
  - This imprecise information is still relevant

Decision factors:
  - Probability of price drop (uncertain)
  - Magnitude of drop (unknown)
  - Value of having laptop now (subjective)
```

**Key Insight**: Imprecise relevant information is better than precise irrelevant information.

## Common Decision Contexts

### 1. Special Order Decisions

**Relevant Costs**:
- Additional materials needed
- Additional labor (if overtime or new hires)
- Additional variable overhead
- Incremental batch costs (setups)

**Irrelevant Costs**:
- Factory rent (doesn't change)
- Existing equipment depreciation
- Salaried manager (if no change)
- Regular production costs (if capacity exists)

### 2. Make vs Buy (Outsourcing)

**Relevant to Make**:
- Materials saved if buy
- Labor saved if buy
- Variable overhead saved
- Equipment opportunity cost (if can be sold/used elsewhere)

**Irrelevant**:
- Allocated fixed overhead (if unavoidable)
- Sunk cost of existing equipment
- Depreciation (unless impacts taxes)

### 3. Product Line Decisions

**Relevant if Discontinue**:
- Lost contribution margin
- Saved product-specific costs
- Saved avoidable fixed costs

**Irrelevant**:
- Allocated facility-level costs (if unavoidable)
- Sunk development costs
- Common costs that continue

### 4. Constrained Resource Decisions

**Relevant**:
- Contribution margin per unit of constraint
- Variable costs that change with volume

**Irrelevant**:
- Fixed costs (same regardless of product mix)
- Allocated overhead

## Analysis Framework

**Step 1**: Define alternatives clearly
**Step 2**: Identify costs/revenues that differ
**Step 3**: Quantify differential amounts
**Step 4**: Consider qualitative factors
**Step 5**: Make decision based on net benefit

### Example Template

```
                        Alternative A    Alternative B    Difference
Relevant Revenues:      $XXX            $XXX             $XXX
Relevant Costs:         $(XXX)          $(XXX)           $XXX
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net Benefit:            $XXX            $XXX             $XXX

Qualitative Factors:
  - Factor 1: [Description]
  - Factor 2: [Description]
  
Recommendation: [Based on analysis]
```

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Including Sunk Costs
```
"We spent $10,000 on this machine, we can't stop using it now"
  - $10,000 is sunk, irrelevant
  - Decision should be: Is continuing better than alternatives?
```

### 2. Ignoring Opportunity Costs
```
"This option has no cost"
  - But are we giving up something valuable?
  - What's the next best alternative?
```

### 3. Allocating Fixed Costs
```
"Product shows loss after allocation of corporate overhead"
  - If overhead continues without product, irrelevant
  - Focus on contribution margin and avoidable costs
```

### 4. Forgetting Qualitative Factors
```
Focusing only on dollars and cents
  - Strategic fit matters
  - Customer relationships matter
  - Employee morale matters
```
