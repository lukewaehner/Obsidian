## Journal Entries

A journal is referred to as the book of original entry. For each transaction, the journal shows the debit and credit effects on specific accounts.

**Journalizing**: Entering transaction data in the journal.

---

## Examples

**Example 1: Issue Stock**

Transaction: Investors invest $10,000 cash in exchange for common stock.

```
Dr. Cash                 10,000
    Cr. Common Stock              10,000
```

**Example 2: Purchase Building with Note**

Transaction: Purchase office building for $500,000 by signing a 36-month, 12% note payable.

```
Dr. Buildings           500,000
    Cr. Notes Payable            500,000
```

At end of month, record interest:

```
Dr. Interest Expense      5,000
    Cr. Cash                      5,000
```

**Example 3: Record Wages**

Transaction: On 10/28, record workers' wages of $20,000, to be paid on 11/02.

On 10/28:

```
Dr. Wages Expense        20,000
    Cr. Wages Payable            20,000
```

On 11/02:

```
Dr. Wages Payable        20,000
    Cr. Cash                     20,000
```

**Example 4: Purchase Equipment on Account**

```
Dr. Equipment             5,000
    Cr. Accounts Payable          5,000
```

**Example 5: Receive Cash for Services**

```
Dr. Cash                 12,000
    Cr. Service Revenue          12,000
```

**Example 6: Bill Client for Services**

```
Dr. Accounts Receivable   8,000
    Cr. Service Revenue           8,000
```

**Example 7: Collect on Account**

```
Dr. Cash                  3,000
    Cr. Accounts Receivable       3,000
```

**Example 8: Pay Dividends**

```
Dr. Dividends             1,000
    Cr. Cash                      1,000
```

**Example 9: Borrow from Bank**

```
Dr. Cash                  9,000
    Cr. Notes Payable             9,000
```
