# Responsibility Accounting

An accounting system that provides information aligned with individual manager responsibilities and controllable costs.

## Core Concept

**Responsibility Accounting**: Provides clearly defined controllable costs that managers can manage and be evaluated against.

**Key Principle**: Managers should only be evaluated on costs and revenues they can **control or strongly influence**.

## Decentralization

**Definition**: The process of pushing decision-making authority down to lower levels in the organization.

### Decentralization Diagram

```
                    CEO
                     |
        ─────────────┴─────────────
        |            |            |
    Division A   Division B   Division C
        |            |            |
    Dept 1-3     Dept 4-6     Dept 7-9
```

**Characteristics**:
- Decisions made by those impacted
- Different managers have different responsibility levels
- Managers held accountable for controllable items
- Promotes faster decision-making

### Benefits of Decentralization

1. **Faster Decisions**: Local managers respond quickly
2. **Better Information**: Managers closer to operations
3. **Manager Development**: Build decision-making skills
4. **Focus**: Top management focuses on strategy
5. **Motivation**: Autonomy increases engagement

### Challenges of Decentralization

1. **Coordination**: Ensuring units work toward common goals
2. **Duplication**: Multiple units may duplicate efforts
3. **Goal Conflict**: Unit goals may conflict with company goals
4. **Control**: Harder for top management to monitor all decisions

## Types of Responsibility Centers

### 1. Cost Center

**Responsibility**: Control costs only

**Evaluation**: Actual costs vs budgeted costs

**Examples**:
- Manufacturing departments
- Service departments (HR, IT)
- Administrative offices

**Manager Controls**:
- Operating costs
- Efficiency and productivity
- Resource usage

### 2. Revenue Center

**Responsibility**: Generate revenues

**Evaluation**: Actual revenues vs budgeted revenues

**Examples**:
- Sales departments
- Marketing divisions

**Manager Controls**:
- Sales volume
- Pricing (sometimes)
- Marketing strategies

### 3. Profit Center

**Responsibility**: Both revenues and costs

**Evaluation**: Actual profit vs budgeted profit

**Examples**:
- Product lines
- Geographic divisions
- Business units

**Manager Controls**:
- Sales and pricing
- Operating costs
- Product mix

### 4. Investment Center

**Responsibility**: Revenues, costs, AND invested capital

**Evaluation**: Return on investment (ROI) or residual income

**Examples**:
- Autonomous divisions
- Subsidiaries
- Major business segments

**Manager Controls**:
- All profit center items PLUS
- Capital expenditures
- Asset deployment
- Investment decisions

## Controllability Concept

**Controllable Cost**: A cost that a manager has the power to determine or strongly influence.

**Key Insight**: "Predominant control" rather than "absolute control"

### Examples of Controllability

**Production Manager**:

| Cost | Controllable? | Reason |
|------|---------------|--------|
| Direct materials usage | Yes | Manager controls efficiency |
| Direct labor overtime | Yes | Manager schedules workers |
| Equipment depreciation | No | Corporate decision |
| Factory insurance | No | Corporate policy |
| Production supplies | Yes | Manager controls usage |

**Store Manager (Retail)**:

| Cost | Controllable? | Reason |
|------|---------------|--------|
| Store employee wages | Yes | Manager schedules and hires |
| Store utilities | Partially | Can control usage, not rates |
| Store rent | No | Corporate lease decision |
| Inventory shrinkage | Yes | Manager controls security |
| Allocated corporate overhead | No | No influence |

### Context Matters

**Regional Supervisor Salary**:
- **Store-level perspective**: Not controllable by store manager
- **Regional perspective**: Directly controllable by regional VP

**Principle**: Controllability depends on organizational level being evaluated.

## Responsibility Reports

**Purpose**: Compare budgeted and actual amounts for controllable items.

### Characteristics of Good Reports

1. **Timely**: Issued promptly (daily, weekly, monthly)
2. **Regular**: Consistent schedule
3. **Understandable**: Clear presentation
4. **Relevant**: Focus on controllable items
5. **Comparative**: Budget vs actual

### Report Format Example

**Production Department - Monthly Performance**

```
                        Budget      Actual    Variance    %
Controllable Costs:
Direct Materials        $50,000    $52,000   $(2,000) U   4%
Direct Labor            $40,000    $39,000    $1,000 F    2%
Supplies                $5,000     $5,500     $(500) U    10%
Utilities               $8,000     $7,800     $200 F      3%
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total Controllable      $103,000   $104,300   $(1,300) U  1%

Non-Controllable:
Depreciation           $10,000    $10,000     $0          0%
Allocated Overhead     $15,000    $15,000     $0          0%
```

**Note**: Report emphasizes controllable costs for manager evaluation.

## Management by Exception

**Definition**: Focus management attention on areas needing additional resources or investigation.

### Report Hierarchy

**Top Management** (CEO):
```
Division A: $(50,000) unfavorable
Division B: $20,000 favorable
Division C: $5,000 favorable
─────────────────────────────────
Total: $(25,000) unfavorable
```
*Sees only summary, requests detail on Division A*

**Division Manager** (Division A):
```
Department 1: $(30,000) unfavorable  ← Investigate
Department 2: $(15,000) unfavorable  ← Investigate
Department 3: $(5,000) unfavorable
─────────────────────────────────────
Total: $(50,000) unfavorable
```

**Department Manager** (Department 1):
```
Materials: $(20,000) unfavorable
Labor: $(8,000) unfavorable
Overhead: $(2,000) unfavorable
```
*Detailed analysis of causes*

**Principle**: Detail increases as you move down organizational levels.

## Return on Investment (ROI)

**Definition**: Ratio of income to investment used to generate that income.

### Basic ROI Formula

```
ROI = Operating Income / Operating Assets

Or as percentage:

ROI = (Operating Income / Operating Assets) × 100%
```

**Purpose**: 
- Measures how effectively assets generate income
- Enables comparison across divisions of different sizes
- Relates profitability to capital employed

### Example: Three Divisions

**Lumber Manufacturing**:
```
Operating Income: $60,000
Operating Assets: $300,000
ROI = $60,000 / $300,000 = 20%
```

**Home Building**:
```
Operating Income: $46,080
Operating Assets: $256,000
ROI = $46,080 / $256,000 = 18%
```

**Furniture Manufacturing**:
```
Operating Income: $81,940
Operating Assets: $482,000
ROI = $81,940 / $482,000 = 17%
```

**Analysis**:
- Lumber has highest ROI despite lowest absolute income
- Furniture has highest income but lowest ROI (inefficient asset use)

### Expanded ROI Formula (DuPont)

**ROI can be disaggregated into margin and turnover**:

```
ROI = Margin × Turnover

Where:
  Margin = Operating Income / Sales
  Turnover = Sales / Operating Assets
```

**Full Formula**:
```
ROI = (Operating Income / Sales) × (Sales / Operating Assets)
    = Operating Income / Operating Assets
```

**Example**:
```
Sales: $600,000
Operating Income: $60,000
Operating Assets: $300,000

Margin = $60,000 / $600,000 = 10%
Turnover = $600,000 / $300,000 = 2.0 times

ROI = 10% × 2.0 = 20%
```

### Benefits of Expanded ROI

**Identifies two paths to improve ROI**:

1. **Increase Margin** (profitability):
   - Increase sales prices
   - Decrease operating costs
   - Improve operating efficiency

2. **Increase Turnover** (asset efficiency):
   - Increase sales with same assets
   - Reduce operating assets
   - Eliminate unproductive assets

**Strategic Choices**:
- **High margin, low turnover**: Luxury goods
- **Low margin, high turnover**: Discount retail (Walmart)

## Residual Income

**Definition**: Operating income above minimum required return on invested capital.

### Formula

```
Residual Income = Operating Income - Investment Charge

Where:
  Investment Charge = Operating Assets × Required Rate of Return
```

**Purpose**: Shows dollar amount returned above minimum.

### Example

**Given**:
- Operating Income: $60,000
- Operating Assets: $300,000
- Required ROI (hurdle rate): 15%

**Calculate**:
```
Investment Charge = $300,000 × 15% = $45,000

Residual Income = $60,000 - $45,000 = $15,000
```

**Interpretation**: Division earned $15,000 more than minimum required return.

### ROI vs Residual Income

**Example Scenario**: Division has 20% ROI, company requires 15% minimum.

**New Investment Opportunity**: Project with 18% expected ROI.

**Using ROI Criterion**:
```
Manager rejects project because:
  18% < 20% (current divisional ROI)
  Would lower division's ROI
```

**Using Residual Income Criterion**:
```
Manager accepts project because:
  18% > 15% (company's minimum)
  Would increase division's residual income
```

**Benefit of Residual Income**: Encourages managers to accept all projects exceeding minimum rate, aligning divisional and company goals.

### Three Division Comparison

| Division | Operating Income | Operating Assets | ROI | Residual Income (15% required) |
|----------|-----------------|------------------|-----|-------------------------------|
| Lumber | $60,000 | $300,000 | 20% | $15,000 |
| Home | $46,080 | $256,000 | 18% | $7,680 |
| Furniture | $81,940 | $482,000 | 17% | $9,640 |

**Analysis**:
- By ROI: Lumber is best (20%)
- By RI: Lumber still best ($15,000), but Furniture shows more absolute value creation

## Behavioral Implications

**Cost allocations affect behavior**:

1. **Performance Evaluations**: Based on reported profits
2. **Compensation**: Often tied to performance metrics
3. **Decision-Making**: Managers optimize their metrics
4. **Resource Allocation**: Corporate allocates based on performance

**Potential Dysfunctional Behavior**:
- Gaming the system
- Short-term focus
- Sub-optimization (good for division, bad for company)
- Reluctance to invest

**Solutions**:
- Multiple performance measures
- Long-term incentives
- Qualitative factors
- Frequent communication
