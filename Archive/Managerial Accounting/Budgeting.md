# Budgeting

The process of planning future operations through formal financial projections.

## Overview

**Budgeting**: Creating a detailed financial plan that describes expected operations for a future period.

**Purpose**: Translate strategic and operational plans into financial terms.

## Levels of Planning

### 1. Strategic Planning

**Time Horizon**: Long-term (3-5+ years)

**Focus**: 
- Scope of business
- Products to develop or discontinue
- Markets to enter or exit
- Most profitable strategies
- Competitive positioning

**Outputs**: Strategic initiatives, major investments

### 2. Capital Expenditure Planning (CAPEX)

**Time Horizon**: Intermediate (1-3 years)

**Focus**:
- Buying or leasing equipment
- Facility expansion
- Technology investments
- Initiatives to stimulate sales
- Asset acquisitions

**Outputs**: Capital budget

### 3. Operations Planning

**Time Horizon**: Short-term (1 year, broken into quarters/months)

**Focus**:
- Specific sales targets
- Production goals
- Detailed resource needs
- Financing requirements

**Outputs**: Operating budget (master budget)

## Strategy Framework

### Organizational Strategy

Three strategic approaches:

1. **Customer Intimacy**: Deep relationships, customization
2. **Product Innovation**: Cutting-edge, first-to-market
3. **Price Leadership**: Low cost, high volume

### Congruence

Strategy must align with:
- **Processes**: How work gets done
- **People**: Skills and capabilities
- **Culture**: Values and behaviors
- **Structure**: Organization design

### Business Model

Components that execute strategy:
- **Go-to-Market**: Sales and distribution channels
- **Operations**: Production and delivery
- **Sales**: Customer acquisition and retention
- **R&D**: Innovation and development

## Advantages of Budgeting

### 1. Promotes Planning

**Forces managers to**:
- Look ahead systematically
- Anticipate problems before they occur
- Coordinate activities across departments
- Think about resources needed

**Example**: Sales forecasts drive production planning, which drives material purchasing.

### 2. Promotes Coordination

**Ensures**:
- All departments work toward common goals
- Resources allocated where needed
- Conflicts identified early
- Integration across functions

**Example**: Production capacity must match sales projections.

### 3. Enhances Performance Measurement

**Provides**:
- Clear targets for evaluation
- Basis for variance analysis
- Accountability framework
- Objective performance standards

**Example**: Compare actual sales to budgeted sales.

### 4. Enables Corrective Action

**Facilitates**:
- Early problem identification
- Timely response to deviations
- Resource reallocation
- Process improvements

**Example**: If actual costs exceed budget, investigate and adjust.

## Budgeting and Human Behavior

### The Challenge

**Budgets constrain**: Limit individual freedom in favor of organizational plan.

**Stress factors**:
- Performance evaluations based on budgets
- Compensation tied to meeting targets
- Pressure to achieve difficult goals
- Fear of negative consequences

### Management Considerations

**Upper management must**:

1. **Demonstrate sincerity**: 
   - Budgets express realistic expectations
   - Not arbitrary or punitive
   - Achievable with effort

2. **Ensure participation**:
   - Involve managers in budget creation
   - Bottom-up input, not just top-down directives
   - Build buy-in and ownership

3. **Provide support**:
   - Resources to achieve targets
   - Training and guidance
   - Recognition of effort

4. **Balance pressure**:
   - Challenging but attainable goals
   - Not impossible stretch targets
   - Room for reasonable variance

### Behavioral Pitfalls

**Budget Slack (Padding)**:
- Managers deliberately underestimate revenues or overestimate costs
- Creates cushion for easier goal achievement
- Reduces budget usefulness

**Gaming the System**:
- Focus on metrics, not real performance
- Short-term actions that hurt long-term
- Manipulation of timing (defer expenses to next period)

**Blame and Politics**:
- Finger-pointing when targets missed
- Political maneuvering for favorable allocations
- Departments competing rather than cooperating

**Solutions**:
- Realistic goal-setting
- Multiple performance measures
- Focus on continuous improvement
- Separate budgets from compensation (partially)

## Operating Budget Components

The master budget consists of several interconnected sub-budgets:

### 1. Sales Budget

**Purpose**: Forecast sales by segment, department, or product.

**Components**:
- Expected unit sales
- Expected selling prices
- Expected collections (cash)

**Example**:
```
                Q1      Q2      Q3      Q4      Year
Units          1,000   1,200   1,500   1,300   5,000
Price          $100    $100    $100    $100    $100
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Revenue        $100K   $120K   $150K   $130K   $500K
```

**Critical**: Sales budget drives all other budgets.

### 2. Production Budget

**Purpose**: Determine units to produce to meet sales and inventory goals.

**Formula**:
```
Required Production = Expected Sales + Desired Ending Inventory - Beginning Inventory
```

**Example**:
```
Expected sales:            1,000 units
Desired ending inventory:    200 units (20% of next period)
Beginning inventory:         150 units
─────────────────────────────────────
Required production:       1,050 units
```

### 3. Direct Materials Budget

**Purpose**: Determine materials to purchase.

**Formula**:
```
Materials Needed = Production × Material per Unit
Purchases = Materials Needed + Desired Ending RM - Beginning RM
```

**Example**:
```
Production:                1,050 units
Material per unit:         2 lbs
Material cost:             $5/lb
─────────────────────────────────────
Materials needed:          2,100 lbs
Desired ending RM:         420 lbs (20% of next period)
Beginning RM:              300 lbs
─────────────────────────────────────
Purchases needed:          2,220 lbs
Cost: 2,220 × $5 =        $11,100
```

### 4. Direct Labor Budget

**Purpose**: Determine labor hours and costs.

**Formula**:
```
Labor Hours = Production × Hours per Unit
Labor Cost = Labor Hours × Hourly Rate
```

**Example**:
```
Production:                1,050 units
Hours per unit:            0.5 hours
Hourly rate:               $20/hour
─────────────────────────────────────
Total hours:               525 hours
Total cost:                $10,500
```

### 5. Manufacturing Overhead Budget

**Purpose**: Budget for all indirect manufacturing costs.

**Categories**:
- Variable overhead (supplies, indirect materials, utilities)
- Fixed overhead (rent, depreciation, insurance)

**Example**:
```
Variable overhead:         $5 per direct labor hour × 525 hrs = $2,625
Fixed overhead:            $20,000 per quarter
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total overhead:            $22,625
```

### 6. Selling & Administrative Expense Budget

**Purpose**: Budget for period costs.

**Categories**:
- Selling expenses (commissions, advertising, shipping)
- Administrative (salaries, office rent, utilities)

**Example**:
```
Sales commissions (5%):    $5,000
Advertising:               $10,000
Admin salaries:            $25,000
Office expenses:           $8,000
─────────────────────────────────────
Total S&A:                 $48,000
```

### 7. Cash Budget

**Purpose**: Ensure adequate cash for operations.

**Components**:
- Beginning cash balance
- Cash receipts (collections from sales)
- Cash disbursements (payments for expenses)
- Financing needs (borrowing or repayment)

**Example**:
```
Beginning cash:            $20,000
Collections:               $95,000
───────────────────────────────────
Cash available:            $115,000

Payments:
  Materials:               $(11,100)
  Labor:                   $(10,500)
  Overhead (cash):         $(12,625)
  S&A (cash):              $(43,000)
───────────────────────────────────
Ending cash:               $37,775
```

### 8. Budgeted Financial Statements

**Budgeted Income Statement**:
- Uses data from all budgets
- Shows expected profitability

**Budgeted Balance Sheet**:
- Shows expected financial position at period end
- Accounts for inventory changes, cash changes, etc.

## Continuous vs Zero-Based Budgeting

### Continuous (Incremental) Budgeting

**Approach**: Start with last year's budget, adjust for changes.

**Advantages**:
- Quick and easy
- Stable and predictable
- Less disruption

**Disadvantages**:
- Perpetuates inefficiencies
- Doesn't question existing spending
- May not align with current strategy

### Zero-Based Budgeting

**Approach**: Start from zero, justify every dollar.

**Advantages**:
- Identifies unnecessary costs
- Aligns with current priorities
- Encourages efficiency

**Disadvantages**:
- Time-consuming
- Resource-intensive
- Can be politically challenging

## Rolling Budgets

**Definition**: Continuously updated budget that adds a new period as current period ends.

**Example**: 
- 12-month budget
- Each month, add new month to the end
- Always have 12 months ahead

**Benefits**:
- Always looking ahead same timeframe
- More current than annual budget
- Encourages continuous planning
