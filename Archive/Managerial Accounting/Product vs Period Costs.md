# Product vs Period Costs

Two fundamental cost classifications based on **timing of expense recognition**.

## Product Costs

**Definition**: All costs related to obtaining or manufacturing a product intended for sale to customers.

**Characteristics**:
- Accumulated in **inventory accounts** (Balance Sheet)
- Expensed as **Cost of Goods Sold** at point of sale (Income Statement)
- Also called **inventoriable costs**

**Components in Manufacturing**:
1. **Direct Materials (DM)**: Raw materials directly traceable to product
2. **Direct Labor (DL)**: Wages of workers who directly make the product
3. **Manufacturing Overhead (MOH)**: Indirect production costs not easily traceable

### Example: Manufacturing a Chair
```
Direct Materials:     Wood, screws, fabric  → Product Cost
Direct Labor:         Assembly worker wages → Product Cost
Manufacturing OH:     Factory rent, glue    → Product Cost
```

## Period Costs

**Definition**: Costs not directly related to production, expensed in the period incurred.

**Characteristics**:
- Expensed immediately (Income Statement)
- No delay in recognition
- Also called **operating expenses**

**Common Period Costs**:
- **Selling expenses**: Sales commissions, advertising, shipping
- **General & Administrative (G&A)**: Office salaries, rent, utilities
- **Research & Development (R&D)**: Product development, testing

### Example: Selling a Chair
```
Sales commission:        → Period Cost (Selling)
Office manager salary:   → Period Cost (G&A)
Advertising:            → Period Cost (Selling)
```

## Cost Flow Comparison

**Product Costs Flow**:
```
Incurred → Inventory (Asset) → COGS (Expense) at sale
```

**Period Costs Flow**:
```
Incurred → Expense immediately
```

## Financial Statement Impact

### Product Costs
```
Balance Sheet:
  Assets: Inventory increases when incurred

Income Statement:
  COGS: Expensed only when product sells
```

### Period Costs
```
Balance Sheet:
  No impact (not capitalized)

Income Statement:
  Operating Expenses: Expensed immediately
```

## Key Principle

**Matching Principle**: Product costs are matched with revenue when the product is sold, while period costs are matched with the period in which they're incurred.

## Decision Context

**Why the distinction matters**:
- Product costs affect inventory valuation
- Period costs are easier to control/cut
- Product costs directly trace to product profitability
- Period costs support overall operations

### Controllability
- **Product costs**: Harder to control (directly tied to production)
- **Period costs**: Easier to control (often discretionary)
