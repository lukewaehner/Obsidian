## Systematic and Unsystematic Risk

- **Systematic Risk** (Market Risk, Non-Diversifiable Risk)
	- What is common to the whole economy

- **Unsystematic Risk** (Unique Risk, Firm-Specific Risk)
	- Risk that influences a single company, or possibly a small group of companies
	- Risk that can be eliminated with diversification

$$
\text{Total Risk} = \text{Systematic Risk} + \text{Unsystematic Risk}
$$

---

## Examples

**Systematic:**

- GDP
- Interest rates
- Inflation
- War
- Pandemic

**Unsystematic:**

- Apple's new product announcement
- Intel being late on product delivery

## Unexpected Return Decomposition

We previously defined:

$$
\text{Unexpected Return} = \text{Total Return} - \text{Expected Return}
$$

Now we can say:

$$
\text{Unexpected Return} = \text{Systematic Portion} + \text{Unsystematic Portion}
$$

Thus, unexpected return for a firm is unexpected events affecting the entire economy and unexpected events affecting only the firm.
