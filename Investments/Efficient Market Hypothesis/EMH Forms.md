
## EMH Forms

The Efficient Market Hypothesis has three forms based on the type of information reflected in prices.

---

### Weak-Form Efficiency

**Definition**: Current prices reflect all **past technical information**.

**Information Included**:
- Historical prices
- Past returns
- Trading volume
- Price patterns and charts

**Implications**:
- Knowing past returns/volume does not help predict future returns
- Charts are useless for predicting future prices
- **Technical analysis is of little use**

**What This Means**:
If weak-form EMH holds, you cannot earn excess returns by analyzing:
- Moving averages
- Support and resistance levels
- Head and shoulders patterns
- Any pattern based on historical price data

---

### Semi-Strong-Form Efficiency

**Definition**: Prices reflect all **publicly available information**.

**Information Included**:
- All technical information (from weak form)
- Accounting data
- Financial statements
- Managerial statements
- Conference calls
- News announcements
- Industry reports

**Implications**:
- Publicly available information is of no use in beating the market
- **Fundamental analysis provides no advantage**
- Stock prices instantly adjust to new public information

**Known Anomalies** (challenges to semi-strong form):
- **P/E effect**: Low P/E stocks tend to outperform
- **January Effect**: Stocks tend to rise in January
- **Neglected firm effect**: Less-followed stocks may be mispriced
- **Book-to-market ratios**: High book-to-market stocks outperform
- **Post-earnings announcement drift**: Prices continue drifting after earnings

**What This Means**:
If semi-strong EMH holds, you cannot consistently beat the market by:
- Reading financial statements
- Analyzing earnings reports
- Following analyst recommendations
- Studying public news

---

### Strong-Form Efficiency

**Definition**: Prices reflect **all information, public or private**.

**Information Included**:
- All publicly available information (from semi-strong)
- **Private/inside information**:
  - R&D outcomes not yet disclosed
  - Merger agreements not yet released
  - Unpublished financial statements
  - Executive decisions
  - Proprietary business intelligence

**Implications**:
- Even **inside information** is of no use
- No investor can consistently earn excess returns
- This is the most extreme form of EMH

**Reality Check**:
Strong-form efficiency is **not observed in practice**:
- Insider trading laws exist because insiders CAN profit
- Corporate insiders do earn excess returns
- This form is more theoretical than practical

---

## Summary Table

| Form | Information Reflected | Can't Beat Market Using | Observed? |
|------|----------------------|-------------------------|-----------|
| **Weak** | Past prices/volume | Technical analysis | Mostly yes |
| **Semi-Strong** | All public information | Fundamental analysis | Mostly yes |
| **Strong** | All information (public + private) | Even insider info | No |

---

## Relationship Between Forms

```
Weak-Form ⊂ Semi-Strong Form ⊂ Strong-Form

If semi-strong holds → weak-form must hold
If strong-form holds → semi-strong and weak must hold
```

**But**:
- Weak-form can hold without semi-strong holding
- Semi-strong can hold without strong-form holding

---

## Testing the Forms

**Weak-Form Tests**:
- Serial correlation in returns
- Trading rule profitability
- Technical pattern success rates

**Semi-Strong Tests**:
- Event studies (how quickly prices react to news)
- Mutual fund performance
- Anomaly investigation

**Strong-Form Tests**:
- Insider trading returns
- Corporate officer trading profits
