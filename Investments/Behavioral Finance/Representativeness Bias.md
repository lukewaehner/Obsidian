## Representativeness Bias

The tendency to find patterns in small or recent samples and incorrectly apply them to the entire population.

---

## How It Works

Investors judge the probability of an event by how similar it is to their mental prototype, rather than by actual statistical likelihood. Small samples are treated as representative of larger populations.

---

## Example

A fund manager has beaten the market for three consecutive years. An investor concludes this manager has superior skill and invests heavily, expecting continued outperformance. However, with thousands of fund managers, some will beat the market three years in a row purely by chance. The investor has mistaken a small sample of returns for evidence of genuine ability. Statistically, most managers who outperform over short periods revert to average performance.

---

## Another Example

A stock has risen 15% each of the last four quarters. An investor assumes the company must be fundamentally superior and extrapolates this growth indefinitely, not recognizing that four quarters is too short to establish a reliable trend and that growth rates typically mean-revert.
