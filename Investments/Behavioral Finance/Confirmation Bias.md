## Confirmation Bias

The tendency to interpret new information in ways that confirm one's prior beliefs, especially when information is ambiguous.

---

## Behavior Patterns

Investors exhibiting confirmation bias will:

- Disregard information that may disprove their views
- Emphasize supporting signals while downplaying contradictory evidence
- Selectively remember information that confirms their thesis
- Seek out news sources and analysts who share their opinion

---

## Example

An investor believes a pharmaceutical company's new drug will be approved by the FDA. When the company releases clinical trial data showing mixed results, the investor focuses on the positive efficacy numbers while dismissing concerns about side effects raised in the same report. They continue reading bullish analyst reports and avoid bearish coverage. Even when the FDA requests additional trials, the investor interprets this as "just a minor delay" rather than a warning sign.

---

## Market Impact

Confirmation bias can lead to:

- Slow reaction to genuinely negative news
- Prolonged holding of losing positions
- Asset bubbles when many investors share the same bullish bias
