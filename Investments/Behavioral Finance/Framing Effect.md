## Framing Effect

The phenomenon where people's risk preferences change based on how choices are presented, even when the underlying economics are identical.

---

## The Pattern

- When facing gains, people are risk-averse (prefer certainty)
- When facing losses, people are risk-seeking (prefer gambles)

This contradicts traditional finance theory, which assumes consistent risk preferences.

---

## Classic Example

**Gain Frame:**
Choice A: Receive $50 for certain
Choice B: Flip a coin for $0 or $100

Most people choose A (the certain $50), demonstrating risk aversion in the domain of gains.

**Loss Frame:**
Choice C: Lose $50 for certain
Choice D: Flip a coin to lose $0 or $100

Most people choose D (the gamble), demonstrating risk-seeking behavior in the domain of losses, even though the expected values are identical.

---

## Investment Application

A financial advisor presents two equivalent strategies differently:

Frame 1: "This strategy has a 70% chance of gaining $10,000."
Frame 2: "This strategy has a 30% chance of losing your $10,000 investment."

Investors respond more favorably to Frame 1, even though both describe the same probability distribution. Advisors and media can influence investor behavior simply by how they present information.
