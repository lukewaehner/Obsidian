# Turnover Rate

**Definition:** The ratio of trading activity in a portfolio to the assets of the portfolio, measuring the fraction of the portfolio that is "replaced" every year.

## Formula

```
Turnover Rate = (Lesser of Purchases or Sales) / Average Portfolio Assets × 100%
```

### Alternative Definition
> "It measures the fraction of the portfolio that is 'replaced' every year."

## Examples

### Example 1: 50% Turnover
- Portfolio value: $100 million
- Sales of securities: $50 million
- Purchases of securities: $50 million
- **Turnover rate = 50%**

**Interpretation:** Half the portfolio was replaced during the year

### Example 2: 100% Turnover
- Portfolio value: $200 million  
- Sales: $200 million
- Purchases: $200 million
- **Turnover rate = 100%**

**Interpretation:** The entire portfolio was replaced during the year

### Example 3: 25% Turnover
- Portfolio value: $500 million
- Sales: $125 million
- Purchases: $125 million
- **Turnover rate = 25%**

**Interpretation:** One-quarter of the portfolio changed

## What Turnover Tells You

### Trading Activity Level
- **Low turnover (< 25%):** Buy-and-hold strategy
- **Moderate turnover (25-75%):** Balanced approach
- **High turnover (75-150%):** Active trading
- **Very high turnover (> 150%):** Aggressive trading

### Investment Philosophy Indicator
- **Index funds:** Very low (often 3-10%)
- **Value funds:** Low to moderate (20-40%)
- **Growth funds:** Moderate (40-80%)
- **Aggressive growth:** High (80-150%+)
- **Market timing funds:** Very high (150%+)

## Reporting Requirements

### Mandatory Disclosure
> "Must report turnover, which is related to the amount of trading"

Funds must disclose turnover rate in:
- Prospectus
- Annual report
- Semi-annual report

### Where to Find It
- Fee table section
- Financial highlights table
- "Portfolio Turnover" line item

## Relationship to Costs

### The Connection
```
Higher Turnover → More Trading → Higher Trading Costs → Lower Returns
```

> "The more trading, the higher the trading costs"

### [[Trading Costs]] Include
1. **Commissions** - Broker fees for trades
2. **Bid-ask spreads** - Market maker profit
3. **Market impact** - Price moves from large orders
4. **Opportunity costs** - Timing of execution

### Important Note
> "Trading costs: Not reported directly, but can find out aggregate changes between quarters/months"

Trading costs are **implicit costs**:
- Not shown as a line item
- Embedded in performance
- Can infer from returns vs. holdings
- Academic studies estimate impact

## Impact on Returns

### Cost Drag
**Estimated trading costs by turnover:**
- 0% turnover: 0% trading cost
- 50% turnover: ~0.5-1.0% cost
- 100% turnover: ~1.0-2.0% cost
- 200% turnover: ~2.0-4.0% cost

*Varies by market capitalization and liquidity*

### Performance Impact
Fund with:
- 10% gross return
- 1% expense ratio
- 100% turnover (1.5% trading cost)
- **= 7.5% net return**

### Compounding Effect
Over 20 years, 1.5% annual trading cost = ~26% less wealth

## Tax Implications

### Taxable Accounts
High turnover generates:
- **Short-term capital gains** (ordinary income tax rates)
- More taxable distributions
- Lower after-tax returns

### Example
- Fund earns 10% total return
- 100% turnover, all short-term gains
- 35% tax bracket
- After-tax return ≈ 6.5%

vs.

- Fund earns 10% total return  
- 10% turnover, mostly long-term gains
- 15% long-term capital gains tax
- After-tax return ≈ 8.5%

### Tax-Advantaged Accounts
Turnover less important in:
- 401(k)
- IRA
- 403(b)
- Other tax-deferred accounts

## Typical Turnover by Fund Type

| Fund Type | Typical Turnover |
|-----------|------------------|
| Index Funds | 3-10% |
| Large-Cap Value | 20-40% |
| Large-Cap Growth | 50-80% |
| Small-Cap | 60-100% |
| Sector Funds | 70-120% |
| International | 40-70% |
| Bond Funds | 50-150% |
| Market Timing | 150-300%+ |

## When High Turnover Is Justified

### Acceptable Scenarios
1. **Small-cap funds** - Need to sell winners that grow too large
2. **Tactical funds** - Strategy requires active trading
3. **Tax-loss harvesting** - In taxable accounts
4. **Fundamental changes** - Company situations deteriorate

### Performance Requirement
High-turnover funds must **outperform enough to justify costs**:
- Need 1-2% extra return to break even
- After costs, should beat low-turnover alternative
- Historical data: Few succeed consistently

## Red Flags

### Warning Signs
- Turnover > 100% for index fund
- Increasing turnover without performance improvement
- High turnover + high expense ratio
- Manager change + turnover spike
- Turnover inconsistent with stated strategy

### Questions to Ask
- Why is turnover so high?
- What are estimated trading costs?
- Does strategy require this much trading?
- How does turnover affect after-tax returns?
- What is turnover trend over time?

## Analyzing Turnover

### Context Matters
Compare turnover to:
1. **Fund's historical average** - Has it changed?
2. **Peer group average** - Is it outlier?
3. **Stated strategy** - Is it consistent?
4. **Performance** - Is it justified?

### Changes in Turnover
**Increasing turnover may indicate:**
- New manager with different style
- Strategy shift
- Market timing attempts
- Window dressing

**Decreasing turnover may indicate:**
- Maturing strategy
- Larger asset base (harder to trade)
- Shift to buy-and-hold
- Lower conviction

## Investor Strategies

### Minimizing Turnover Impact

**In Taxable Accounts:**
1. **Prefer low-turnover funds** (< 30%)
2. **Use tax-managed funds**
3. **Consider index funds**
4. **Hold individual stocks** (control timing)

**In Tax-Advantaged Accounts:**
1. Turnover less critical
2. Focus on gross returns
3. Higher turnover acceptable if performance warrants

### Portfolio Location Strategy
- **Taxable accounts:** Low-turnover funds
- **Tax-deferred accounts:** High-turnover funds

## Limitations of Turnover Metric

### What It Doesn't Show
1. **Timing of trades** - Spread throughout year?
2. **Size of trades** - Small or large positions?
3. **Success of trades** - Profitable or not?
4. **Trading efficiency** - Good or poor execution?

### Additional Research Needed
- Look at actual trading costs (if estimated)
- Check performance net of all costs
- Compare to low-turnover alternative
- Consider tax efficiency
