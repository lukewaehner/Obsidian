# Back-End Load

**Definition:** An "exit" fee charged when redeeming (selling) mutual fund shares.

Also called: **Contingent Deferred Sales Charge (CDSC)**, **Redemption Fee**, **Exit Fee**

## Key Characteristics

### When Charged
- **At the time of redemption** (when you sell)
- One-time fee
- Deducted from proceeds you receive

### How It Works
```
Redemption Value = Shares × NAV
Back-End Load Fee = Redemption Value × CDSC Rate
Proceeds Received = Redemption Value - Back-End Load Fee
```

**Example:**
- You redeem shares worth $10,000
- Back-end load is 1%
- Load charge = $10,000 × 1% = $100
- Proceeds received = $10,000 - $100 = $9,900

### Purpose
- Discourages short-term trading
- Compensates fund for selling costs
- Alternative to front-end loads

## Declining Schedule Structure

### Typical CDSC Schedule
**Key Feature:** Charges **decrease over time**

| Years Held | CDSC Rate |
|-----------|-----------|
| < 1 year | 5% |
| 1-2 years | 4% |
| 2-3 years | 3% |
| 3-4 years | 2% |
| 4-5 years | 1% |
| 5+ years | 0% |

### Common Pattern
> "Funds that impose back-end loads may reduce them by **1 percentage point for every year** the funds are left invested."

### Holding Period Calculation
- Based on **when shares were purchased**
- First-in, first-out (FIFO) typically applies
- Older shares redeemed first (lower/no CDSC)

## Share Class Context

### [[Share Classes|Class C Shares]]
- Typically have **back-end loads** (1% for first year)
- **Higher annual 12b-1 fees** (up to 1%)
- Better for **short-term or uncertain holders**

### Class C Characteristics
- No front-end load
- 1% back-end load if sold within 1 year
- 1% annual 12b-1 fee
- Converts to Class A after ~8-10 years (sometimes)

### Comparison
| Share Class | Front Load | Back Load | Annual 12b-1 | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Class A | 4.5% | 0% | 0.25% | Long-term |
| Class C | 0% | 1% (1 yr) | 1% | Short-term/Uncertain |
| Class B | 0% | 5% (declining) | 0.75% | Medium-term (less common now) |

## Class B Shares (Historical Note)

### Structure
- No front-end load
- High back-end load (starts at 5-6%)
- Declines to 0% over 6-8 years
- Converts to Class A shares after surrender period
- Higher annual expenses during surrender period

### Current Status
- **Largely phased out** by most fund companies
- Replaced by Class C shares
- Existing Class B shares still exist but few new sales
- Regulatory scrutiny led to discontinuation

## CDSC vs. Front-End Load

### Strategic Differences

**Front-End Load (Class A):**
- Pay upfront
- Lower ongoing costs
- Better for long-term (10+ years)

**Back-End Load (Class C):**
- No upfront cost
- Higher ongoing costs
- Better for short-term (< 5 years)

### Break-Even Analysis

Compare Class A (5% front load, 0.25% 12b-1) vs. Class C (1% back load year 1, 1% 12b-1):

**Year 1:**
- Class A: -5% load, -0.25% 12b-1 = -5.25%
- Class C: -1% load, -1% 12b-1 = -2% (if sold)

**Year 5:**
- Class A: -5% load + (-0.25% × 5) = -6.25% total
- Class C: 0% load + (-1% × 5) = -5% total

**Year 10:**
- Class A: -5% + (-0.25% × 10) = -7.5% total
- Class C: 0% + (-1% × 10) = -10% total

Class C becomes more expensive after ~7-8 years.

## Exemptions and Exceptions

### Common Exemptions
- **Systematic withdrawals** - Regular distribution plans
- **Required minimum distributions (RMDs)** - From retirement accounts
- **Death or disability** - Waived in hardship cases
- **Return of excess contributions** - To retirement accounts
- **Free withdrawal amount** - Often 10-15% annually

### Exchanges Within Fund Family
- May avoid CDSC when switching between funds
- Check prospectus for specific rules
- Holding period may carry over

## Advantages of Back-End Loads

### For Investors
1. **Full investment works immediately** - No upfront drag
2. **Decreases over time** - Rewards loyalty
3. **Discourages market timing** - Reduces trading

### For Fund Companies
1. **Retains assets** - Reduces redemptions
2. **Recovers distribution costs** over time
3. **Compensates advisors** without upfront fee

## Disadvantages of Back-End Loads

### Investor Concerns
1. **Higher ongoing costs** - 12b-1 fees add up
2. **Liquidity penalty** - Cost to exit if needed
3. **Complexity** - Declining schedule calculations
4. **Long-term disadvantage** - Eventually costs more

### Regulatory Concerns
- Can trap investors in poorly performing funds
- Incentivizes advisors to sell Class B/C inappropriately
- Less transparent than front-end loads

## Alternatives

### No-Load Funds
- No front-end or back-end loads
- Lower 12b-1 fees or none
- Sold directly by fund companies

### Class I/Institutional Shares
- No loads
- No 12b-1 fees
- Minimum investment requirements ($1M-$5M+)

### ETFs
- No loads
- Trade like stocks
- Commission-free at many brokers

## Required Disclosures

### Prospectus Must Include
- CDSC schedule (rates by holding period)
- Calculation methodology
- Exemptions and exceptions
- Share class comparison table

### Sales Materials
- Must show impact of CDSC
- Include fee examples
- Compare to other share classes
