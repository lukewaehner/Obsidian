

**Definition:** An investment company that stands ready to buy and sell shares in itself to investors at any time, at [[Net Asset Value (NAV)]].

## Key Characteristics

### Share Transactions
- **Purchases:** Shares are sold by the mutual fund to investors at NAV
- **Redemptions:** Investors sell shares back to the fund for cash at NAV
- Fund continuously issues new shares and redeems existing shares

### Pricing
```
Price = NAV
```
- All transactions occur at the fund's net asset value
- No premium or discount to NAV

### Share Supply
- Number of shares outstanding fluctuates based on investor demand
- Fund grows by issuing new shares
- Fund shrinks when investors redeem shares

## Closing to New Investors

### "Closed" Funds (not Closed-End)
If an open-end fund gets too large, it may stop taking new investors:
- Analogy: *"Busy doctor treats existing patients without taking in new patients"*
- The fund is then called a **"closed fund"**
- **Important:** Still takes more money from current investors
- **Important:** Current investors can still withdraw money

### Key Distinction
- A "closed" open-end fund ≠ [[Closed-End Funds|Closed-end fund]]
- Number of shares in a closed fund can still fluctuate as existing owners buy and sell
- See: [[Closed Funds vs Closed-End Funds]]

## Market Share
- **Majority** of all mutual funds are open-end funds
- Most investor assets are in open-end funds

## Comparison

| Feature | Open-End Fund |
|---------|---------------|
| Share Issuance | Continuous - issues and redeems |
| Trading Mechanism | Directly with fund |
| Pricing | Always at NAV |
| Share Count | Variable |
