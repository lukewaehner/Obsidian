

**Critical Distinction:** These terms sound similar but refer to completely different concepts.

## Closed Fund (Closed to New Investors)

### What It Is
- An [[Open-End Funds|open-end fund]] that has stopped accepting new investors
- The fund structure remains open-end

### Why Funds Close
- Fund has grown too large to manage effectively
- Manager wants to maintain investment strategy without dilution
- Performance may suffer if fund gets too big

### What Still Happens
- ✅ **Existing investors can add more money**
- ✅ **Existing investors can withdraw money**
- ✅ **Number of shares fluctuates** as owners buy and sell
- ❌ **New investors cannot open accounts**

### Analogy
*"Like a busy doctor who treats existing patients without taking in new patients"*

## Closed-End Fund

### What It Is
- A [[Closed-End Funds|fundamentally different fund structure]]
- NOT an open-end fund at all

### Characteristics
- **Fixed** number of shares forever
- Shares trade on stock exchanges
- Price can differ from NAV
- No redemptions with the fund
- No new share issuances

## Quick Comparison

| Aspect | Closed Fund | Closed-End Fund |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Base Structure** | Open-end fund | Different structure entirely |
| **Status** | Temporary policy | Permanent structure |
| **Share Count** | Still fluctuates | Fixed forever |
| **New Investors** | Not accepted | Buy on exchange |
| **Existing Investors** | Can add/withdraw | Must trade on exchange |
| **Redemptions** | Yes, with fund | No, only market trading |

## Why the Confusion?

Both terms use the word "close/closed" but mean different things:
- **"Closed" fund** → Closed to new accounts (like a closed door)
- **"Closed-end" fund** → Shares not issued/redeemed on-demand (like a closed system)

## Key Takeaway

When you hear a fund is "closed":
1. First determine: Is this an open-end fund that stopped taking new investors?
2. Or is this a closed-end fund (different structure)?
3. The context usually makes it clear, but always verify
