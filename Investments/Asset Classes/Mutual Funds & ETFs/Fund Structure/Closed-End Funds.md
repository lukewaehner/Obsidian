

**Definition:** An investment company with a fixed number of shares that are bought and sold by investors only in the open market (like stocks).

## Key Characteristics

### Fixed Share Structure
- **Fixed number of shares** - does not change
- Fund does NOT:
  - Accept new money → issue new shares
  - Redeem existing shares → give money back to investors

### Trading Mechanism
- Shares trade on stock exchanges (like common stocks)
- Investors buy from and sell to other investors
- This is how investors get their money back (not through redemption with the fund)

### Pricing
```
Price ≠ NAV
```
- Share prices can differ from [[Net Asset Value (NAV)]]
- Typically sells at a **discount** relative to NAV
  - Discount is sometimes substantial
  - Typical discount fluctuates over time

## The Closed-End Fund Discount Phenomenon

### The Mystery
> "Despite a great deal of academic research, the closed-end fund discount phenomenon remains largely unexplained."

- Closed-end funds typically trade below their NAV
- The size of the discount varies over time
- No widely accepted explanation exists
- This is one of the puzzles in financial economics

See: [[Closed-End Fund Discount Phenomenon]]

## Market Share
- **Small percentage** of all mutual funds/investment trusts
- Less popular than [[Open-End Funds]]

## Comparison with Open-End

| Feature | Closed-End Fund | Open-End Fund |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| Share Issuance | Fixed number | Continuous issuance/redemption |
| Trading | On exchange | Directly with fund |
| Pricing | Price ≠ NAV (often discount) | Price = NAV |
| Liquidity | Depends on market | Always liquid with fund |

## Important Distinction
- Do not confuse with [[Closed Funds vs Closed-End Funds|"closed" open-end funds]]
- A closed-end fund has fundamentally different structure
- "Closed-end" is about the fund's permanent structure, not temporary status
