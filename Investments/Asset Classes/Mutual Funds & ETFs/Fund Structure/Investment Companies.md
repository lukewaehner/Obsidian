# Investment Companies

**Definition:** A business that specializes in pooling funds from individual investors and making investments on their behalf.

## Key Characteristics

### Purpose
- Pool resources from many individual investors
- Provide professional investment management
- Offer diversification that individuals couldn't achieve alone
- Act as [[Financial Intermediary|financial intermediaries]]

### Relationship to Mutual Funds
- A [[Mutual Funds & ETFs|mutual fund]] is simply a corporation (a type of investment company)
- Owned by shareholders who elect a board of directors
- The investment company structure provides the legal framework for mutual funds

### Types of Investment Companies
Investment companies can be organized as:
- [[Open-End Funds]] - Most common structure
- [[Closed-End Funds]] - Fixed share structure

## Management Structure

Investment companies are typically:
- **Created by:** [[Investment Advisory Firms]] (e.g., Fidelity) or brokerage firms with investment advisory operations (e.g., Merrill Lynch)
- **Managed by:** Professional [[Fund Managers]]
- **Governed by:** Board of directors elected by shareholders

### Multiple Funds
- One company can manage many different mutual funds
- Example: Fidelity manages hundreds of different funds

## Similar Financial Intermediaries
Investment companies serve a similar role to:
- Commercial banks
- Life insurance companies
